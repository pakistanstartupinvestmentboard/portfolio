About

A CS student who got curious about attention.

I study Computer Science at LUMS, and somewhere between data structures and a marketing elective I noticed something: the best product rarely wins. The one people already recognize does.

So I started working where code meets go-to-market. I source and qualify leads, define who the buyer actually is, write the messaging, and then automate everything that shouldn't need a human, with tools like Apollo, Salesforce, n8n and GoHighLevel.

Today I do that for companies in the US, UK, Canada and Pakistan, including through my own demand generation practice.

WHAT I STUDY

Attention has quietly become a line of credit. Before any deal, hire, or client conversation starts, someone is deciding, in a few seconds, looking at a name and a few lines of text, whether to keep paying attention. Most founders treat that decision as someone else's problem. I think it's the actual business. A stranger ignores a cold message. The same stranger replies if they already half-recognize the name. That's not a coincidence. It's a pattern, one most people notice only after losing a few deals to it. I spend my time studying that pattern.
